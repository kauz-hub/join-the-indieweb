# 8. Find people and join the community

[Home](../README.md) · [Next: care for your site](09-care-for-your-site.md)

The personal web is held together by people linking to each other. Discovery takes a little more effort than scrolling a social feed, but it can be a lot more rewarding.

## IndieWeb community

- [IndieWeb events](https://indieweb.org/events): find meetups and gatherings.
- [Homebrew Website Club](https://indieweb.org/Homebrew_Website_Club): friendly meetings centered on personal sites.
- [IndieWebCamp](https://indieweb.org/IndieWebCamp): community events for sharing ideas and making things.
- [Chat](https://indieweb.org/discuss): ask questions and meet people.
- [Code of Conduct](https://indieweb.org/code-of-conduct): read before taking part in official spaces.
- [Wiki](https://indieweb.org/): learn from other people's real implementations and share your own once you have one.

## Explore the wider personal web

- [32-Bit Cafe](https://32bit.cafe/): community for personal websites and web weaving.
- [32-Bit Cafe resources](https://32bit.cafe/resources/): learning and design resources.
- [Neocities browse](https://neocities.org/browse): discover handmade sites.
- [ooh.directory](https://ooh.directory/): a curated blog directory across many topics.
- [Wiby](https://wiby.me/): search engine focused on the classic and smaller web.
- [The Old Net](https://theoldnet.com/): explore older web culture and archived sites.
- [IndieWeb directory wiki](https://indieweb.org/directory): additional directories and history.
- [Personal Sites](https://personalsit.es/): personal website discovery.
- [The Rookery](https://therookery.blog/): explore independent blogs and their writing.

Check each directory's submission rules. Some are curated, some require updates or feeds, others accept almost any personal site.

## Make your own connections

A blogroll is a list of blogs or sites you enjoy. Webrings link member sites in a group so readers can explore neighboring sites. An 88×31 button is a small graphic linking to another site, often seen on handmade homepages.

Start a blogroll with a short description of why you like each site. Ask before reusing someone's art when there is no clear permission. Join a webring when its rules fit you, and test the navigation. Link to other writers when responding to their work.

Learn more: [blogroll](https://indieweb.org/blogroll), [webring](https://indieweb.org/webring), [linking](https://indieweb.org/link).

You don't have to submit your site anywhere. You can follow along quietly, write for yourself, or share only with people you trust.
