10. Blogging, microblogging and other ways to publish

A blog is a website or section of a website where someone publishes dated entries. It can be a journal, an essay collection, a project log or a record of everyday life. A microblog uses shorter posts, often a few sentences, a link, a photo or a passing thought.

What should you make?

Format Example Good for
Personal blog An essay about a book you finished Longer reflections and searchable archives
Microblog A sentence about today's weather Casual updates, conversation, bookmarks
Photoblog A photo and a few words Photography and visual diaries
Digital garden Notes that change as you learn Connected ideas without a strict timeline
Weblog / linkblog A link with your own commentary Sharing discoveries and recommendations
Newsletter An occasional email with an online archive Reaching readers directly
Vlog or podcast A video or audio episode Multimedia storytelling
Tumblelog Short mixed-media posts Quotes, images, links and notes in one stream

Long posts and short notes can coexist on the same site. You can create distinct archive pages, tags or feeds for them. Give each post a stable URL so that other people can link directly to it.

Where to write

Service Best fit
Bear Blog Minimal personal blogs with Markdown writing
Micro.blog Short posts, blogs and social connections
Write.as Focused writing
WriteFreely / Paper.wf Federated long-form publishing; check registration and hosting policies
Listed Publishing through Standard Notes' blogging ecosystem
weblog.lol A small publishing service in the omg.lol ecosystem
Ghost Blogs and newsletters
WordPress More control over content and plugins
Neocities / Nekoweb Handmade diaries and microblogs using HTML
Telegraph Quick standalone articles; less suited to a portable personal archive
Tumblr Mixed-media blogging and social discovery; hosted platform rather than full content ownership

Check custom domains, exports, feed support, privacy controls and fees before committing to a platform.

Find your writing rhythm

  • Start with a first post explaining why you created a site.
  • Write short notes when you don't have time for an essay.
  • Keep a backlog of questions, observations and links you want to explore.
  • Link to other writers and respond with your own posts.
  • Consider a challenge, such as Listed's 100-day writing challenge, without turning it into an obligation.
