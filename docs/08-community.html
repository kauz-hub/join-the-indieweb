Chapter 08 · Field guide

8. Find people and join the community

The personal web is held together by people linking to each other. Discovery takes a little more effort than scrolling a social feed, but it can be a lot more rewarding.

IndieWeb community

  • IndieWeb events: find meetups and gatherings.
  • Homebrew Website Club: friendly meetings centered on personal sites.
  • IndieWebCamp: community events for sharing ideas and making things.
  • Chat: ask questions and meet people.
  • Code of Conduct: read before taking part in official spaces.
  • Wiki: learn from other people's real implementations and share your own once you have one.

Explore the wider personal web

  • 32-Bit Cafe: community for personal websites and web weaving.
  • 32-Bit Cafe resources: learning and design resources.
  • Neocities browse: discover handmade sites.
  • ooh.directory: a curated blog directory across many topics.
  • Wiby: search engine focused on the classic and smaller web.
  • The Old Net: explore older web culture and archived sites.
  • IndieWeb directory wiki: additional directories and history.
  • Personal Sites: personal website discovery.
  • The Rookery: explore independent blogs and their writing.

Check each directory's submission rules. Some are curated, some require updates or feeds, others accept almost any personal site.

Make your own connections

A blogroll is a list of blogs or sites you enjoy. Webrings link member sites in a group so readers can explore neighboring sites. An 88×31 button is a small graphic linking to another site, often seen on handmade homepages.

Start a blogroll with a short description of why you like each site. Ask before reusing someone's art when there is no clear permission. Join a webring when its rules fit you, and test the navigation. Link to other writers when responding to their work.

Learn more: blogroll, webring, linking.

You don't have to submit your site anywhere. You can follow along quietly, write for yourself, or share only with people you trust.
