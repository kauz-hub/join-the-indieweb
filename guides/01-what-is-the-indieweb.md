# 1. What is the IndieWeb?

[Home](../README.md) · [Next: choose a platform](02-choose-a-platform.md)

Think of your website as your home base. A social profile can help people find you, but your own address is where you can keep your writing, art, links and public identity over time.

The IndieWeb's [principles](https://indieweb.org/principles) encourage control of your domain and data, publishing useful human-readable pages, building things that meet your needs, and sharing what you learn.

## What ownership really means

- **Identity:** register a domain in your own account if you can. Don't rely on an address that disappears when a platform changes its rules.
- **Content:** keep copies of your posts, images and metadata in formats you can move elsewhere.
- **Distribution:** publish at your own URL, then share it on services where your friends are.
- **Interoperability:** use open formats and protocols when they help you connect with other websites.

Owning a domain doesn't mean owning the entire internet infrastructure. Registrars and hosts are still services. The practical advantage is being able to change providers while keeping the same address and your own copies.

## Related communities and ideas

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Small web | The broader ecosystem of independent, often small-scale sites and human-curated discovery. |
| Personal web | Sites made for someone's interests, identity or creative work. |
| IndieWeb | A community and set of practices centered on personal domains, data ownership and interoperability. |
| Fediverse | Independent servers that communicate, commonly using ActivityPub. Your personal site can connect to it but need not do so. |
| Digital garden | A collection of evolving notes and ideas, often organized by topic rather than publication date. |

You don't need retro graphics, a programming framework, a fediverse account or every IndieWeb protocol. Your website can be plain, colorful, tiny, sprawling, anonymous or public-facing.

Further reading: [Why IndieWeb?](https://indieweb.org/why), [Getting Started](https://indieweb.org/Getting_Started), [IndieWeb principles](https://indieweb.org/principles).
